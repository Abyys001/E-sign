# Legal / jurisdiction system

> A jurisdiction profile selects **drafting variants** and surfaces **things a lawyer must confirm**. Selecting one does not make a contract compliant.

Profiles (`src/server/legal/jurisdictions.ts`, in code so they are reviewed like code): England & Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, Germany, France, Netherlands, Spain, UAE, US-NY, US-CA, and `OTHER` (generic variants plus a non-standard-jurisdiction flag).

## Signing policy (`signing-policy.ts`)
`evaluateSigningPolicy` compares what the agreement needs (witness, deed, physical presence, advanced/qualified signature, certificate, trusted timestamp, extra formalities) with `PLATFORM_CAPABILITIES`, which is **simple electronic signature only**. If anything else is needed the result is *"Additional execution formalities required."*:
- Option A never auto-sends (`generationStatus = HALTED`);
- staff cannot approve it for e-signature (`approveForSignature` refuses);
- the contract is still generated as a draft for manual handling.
The platform never simulates AES/QES, witnessing or a deed.

## Legal-review flags (`review-flags.ts`)
Warnings, not blockers: non-standard jurisdiction, unreviewed or in-review clauses, custom refund/liability/termination, consumer-facing terms, exclusivity, policy conflicts, formalities. Every generated contract stores them in `contracts.legal_flags`.

## Research matrix
`docs/legal/research-matrix.md` (generated by `npx tsx scripts/gen-legal-matrix.ts` from `src/server/legal/data/matrix.json`): 213 rows for UK, EU, US, UAE and others. **Only 7 rows were verified against the source text, 42 partially, 164 are unverified background knowledge.** Most official sites blocked automated fetching. Rows carry `verification` and `lastVerified` (null when nothing was read). It is a lead list for counsel, not a compliance statement. A test asserts unique ids, sources, honest dates, and no compliance claims.
